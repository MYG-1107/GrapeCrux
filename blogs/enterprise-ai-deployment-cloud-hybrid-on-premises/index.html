Enterprise AI deployment: cloud, hybrid or on-premises?

The right AI deployment model depends on data, users, performance targets and operating capability—not a blanket preference for cloud or on-premises infrastructure. Use this decision framework to choose a proportionate architecture.

Start with workload requirements

Classify the information models will process, the regions where it may be stored or processed, who needs access and the consequences of interruption. Record latency, throughput, availability, audit and retention requirements. Identify whether the use case needs hosted models, open-weight models, private retrieval, fine-tuning or a combination.

Write down prohibited outcomes too: public exposure of internal endpoints, uncontrolled cross-border processing, training on restricted customer data or direct model access to production systems.

Understand the deployment choices

A managed public-cloud AI service can reduce infrastructure operations and provide managed models and scaling. A private or dedicated deployment provides more control over network boundaries and data paths but still has provider responsibilities. Hybrid designs keep selected data or services private while using cloud models through controlled connections. On-premises deployments can meet specific isolation, connectivity or latency requirements, but require specialist hardware, patching, capacity and model operations.

Build a decision matrix and validate identity

Assess data sensitivity, regional control, network exposure, operational maturity, model capability, cost predictability, hardware availability and recovery. Separate data residency from broader sovereignty questions: storage location alone does not explain who can access data, support arrangements, legal obligations or key control. Verify these with legal and security teams.

For sensitive systems, consider private endpoints, restricted ingress and egress, segmentation, workload identity and separate service accounts. A service account that can read an index should not automatically inherit every user’s access.

Model total cost, then pilot

Compare model pricing plus GPUs, storage, vector indexes, network egress, logging, security, support, redundancy and operations staffing. Estimate both peak and average demand and test growth assumptions. Run a representative pilot with production-like data classifications and measure end-to-end latency, task success, recovery and cost.

  • Classify data and set mandatory processing constraints.
  • Define latency, throughput, availability and recovery targets.
  • Compare managed, private, hybrid and on-premises options.
  • Validate network, identity, logging and encryption controls.
  • Document shared responsibility, portability and exit options.
