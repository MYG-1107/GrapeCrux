How to scope a useful AI assistant

A chat window alone does not make a reliable AI product. Start with one well-defined task, the information the assistant is allowed to use, and the point where a human should take over.

1. Choose one clear task

Decide what users are trying to accomplish: find a product answer, understand a service, locate a policy, summarise approved documents or prepare a support request. Keep the first version narrow enough to evaluate.

2. Establish a source of truth

List the documents and systems the assistant may use, assign an owner to keep them accurate, and identify information it must never disclose. An answer based on stale or unapproved content can be worse than no answer.

3. Define boundaries and escalation

Specify when the assistant should say it does not know, ask a follow-up question or route a request to a person. Do not let it invent prices, delivery dates, legal promises, company credentials or customer-specific facts.

4. Design security and privacy early

Keep provider keys on a backend, minimise the personal information collected, validate inputs, rate-limit requests and define retention. Restrict system access to what the use case truly needs.

5. Test real-world behaviour

Build a test set that includes ordinary questions, incomplete requests, contradictory information, unsupported questions and attempts to make the assistant reveal protected details. Measure useful task completion and safe fallback—not just how natural the responses sound.

Quick checklist

  • One clearly defined task
  • Approved knowledge sources and content owner
  • Known unsupported questions
  • Human handoff or fallback
  • Server-side secrets and request controls
  • Test cases, monitoring and review process
Keep it grounded: adapt this checklist to your organisation, technical setup and legal obligations. The right scope depends on the actual use case and requirements.
