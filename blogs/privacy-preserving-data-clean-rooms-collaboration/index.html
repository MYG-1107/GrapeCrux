Data clean rooms: safer collaboration without uncontrolled data sharing

Organizations want to analyze overlapping datasets without exchanging raw customer lists or unrestricted source data. A data clean room can help, but only when identity, permitted queries, output controls and governance are designed together.

A clean room is a governed data product

A data clean room is a controlled environment where approved parties analyze datasets under agreed rules. It can support audience overlap analysis, measurement, research collaboration or supply-chain insight without giving each participant unrestricted access to raw records.

The term does not guarantee anonymity or legal compliance. Poorly designed query tools can reveal people through small groups, repeated queries, differencing or external datasets. Treat the environment as a governed data product.

Define purpose, parties and permitted questions

Document the business purpose, participant roles, source provenance, allowed attributes, retention and deletion process. Agree which queries are permitted and what outputs can leave the environment. Use approved schemas and query templates for common tasks; review new query patterns that introduce granular dimensions or small cohorts.

A participant should not be able to use the clean room as a general-purpose export tool. Review contractual obligations and the applicable privacy basis with qualified advisers.

Apply privacy controls to outputs

Use minimum cohort thresholds, aggregation, suppression and rate limits where appropriate. Assess whether repeated queries can reveal a hidden record. Consider differential privacy or other formal techniques when the use case and statistical requirements support them, and make the effect on analytical utility clear.

Review outputs for identifiers and rare combinations. Test controls with adversarial query sequences, not just one query. Maintain records of request, policy decision, output and reviewer.

Build separation and accountability

Separate participant identities, ingestion roles, query execution identities and administrators. Encrypt data and isolate workloads. Apply least privilege to storage, catalogs and compute. Maintain lineage from source through normalization, joins and aggregation. Define how a participant or dataset is revoked when a contract or consent condition changes.

  • Specify purpose and approved analytical questions.
  • Inventory source provenance, sensitivity and legal basis.
  • Restrict query shapes and inspect potentially identifying outputs.
  • Test small-cohort and repeated-query risks.
  • Validate deletion, expiry and participant offboarding.
