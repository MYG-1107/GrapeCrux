How we approach the work

Each project is scoped around its users, systems, dependencies and acceptance criteria. The steps below describe a typical working pattern; the final plan is agreed with the client.

01

Choose one process and describe its inputs, outputs, users and exceptions.

02

Measure current effort and identify what should remain under human control.

03

Confirm source systems, permissions, APIs, privacy and failure handling.

04

Implement a limited workflow and test normal, duplicate and failure cases.

05

Agree ownership, monitoring and change control before wider rollout.

Security, quality and ownership

Access should follow least-privilege principles. Data flows, error handling, testing responsibilities, documentation, handover and post-launch ownership should be agreed before delivery. Regulated or high-impact use cases need an additional review of applicable obligations and qualified oversight.

Frequently asked questions

Can every business process be automated?

No. Processes with unclear ownership, unstable rules or sensitive judgement may need redesign or human review before automation.

Can workflows connect to our current CRM or ERP?

This depends on the available APIs, permissions, licensing, data model and vendor restrictions. Those constraints are reviewed during discovery.

How do you prevent duplicate actions?

Use idempotency keys or transaction references where possible, validate source events and define safe retry and reconciliation procedures.
