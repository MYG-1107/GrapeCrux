Site reliability engineering: service levels, error budgets and incident learning

A practical introduction to SLOs, SLIs, error budgets, incident response and post-incident learning for software teams.

By GrapeCrux Editorial Team

Reliability is not the absence of every failure. It is the ability to deliver an agreed level of service, detect problems and recover in a controlled way. Site reliability engineering provides a framework for balancing delivery speed with service stability. Teams can use its ideas without adopting a large organisation's entire operating model.

Define what users experience

Start with user-facing indicators such as successful requests, latency, task completion or data freshness. Avoid selecting metrics simply because they are easy to collect. A server can report healthy while users cannot complete a purchase or submit an important form.

Set service level objectives

An SLO describes a target for a service-level indicator over a defined window. Choose a target based on business impact and realistic measurement. Do not promise 100 percent availability without accounting for dependencies, maintenance and failure modes. Document exclusions and how measurements are calculated.

Use error budgets as a decision aid

An error budget represents the allowed unreliability within the chosen objective. When the service consumes it quickly, the team may need to prioritise reliability work before adding risky changes. The policy should be agreed in advance and used to guide decisions, not to punish individuals for incidents.

Prepare a practical incident process

Define severity levels, incident ownership, communication channels and escalation routes. Give responders access to current architecture diagrams, dashboards, runbooks and rollback steps. Practise failure scenarios so teams know how to coordinate when a real incident interrupts normal work.

Learn without blame

After an incident, document customer impact, timeline, contributing conditions, detection gaps and recovery actions. Avoid reducing the analysis to an individual mistake. Identify system changes that would make the failure less likely or less damaging, assign owners and review completion.

Improve the whole service

Track repeat incidents, recovery time, alert quality, deployment failure rates and user impact. Reliability work includes dependencies, data pipelines, identity, network paths, support processes and vendor services—not just application servers.

Key takeaway: Use SLOs and error budgets to make reliability trade-offs explicit, then turn incidents into system improvements.
