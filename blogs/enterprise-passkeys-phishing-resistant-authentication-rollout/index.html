Enterprise passkeys: a practical rollout for phishing-resistant sign-in

Passwords and one-time codes remain vulnerable to phishing and account takeover. Passkeys can strengthen authentication, but a successful rollout needs careful identity inventory, recovery design, device support and a plan for privileged accounts.

Why phishing resistance matters

Passwords can be captured by fake login pages. One-time codes can also be relayed or stolen through real-time phishing. Passkeys use public-key cryptography and are designed to bind authentication to the legitimate relying party, reducing the effectiveness of common credential-phishing techniques.

CISA’s mobile communications best-practice guidance recommends FIDO phishing-resistant authentication and identifies passkeys as an acceptable alternative to hardware security keys in the contexts it discusses. Enterprise decisions should still consider identity architecture, threat model and workforce needs.

Inventory accounts before changing sign-in

Start with the identity provider, applications, administrators, remote access, email, finance systems, developer platforms and third-party accounts. Identify which support FIDO2 or passkeys and which still rely on passwords, SMS or weaker fallbacks. Prioritize privileged and high-value accounts.

Document device ownership, supported authenticators and recovery options. Track service identities separately; a human passkey rollout does not solve machine credential exposure.

Design enrollment and recovery together

Pilot with remote workers, contractors, help-desk staff and users with different device types. Explain enrollment, backup authenticators and recovery when a device is lost. Recovery must not undermine the primary control; avoid help-desk processes that bypass passkeys through weak identity checks.

Provide approved backup authenticators for administrators and critical roles. Stage enforcement and give temporary exceptions an owner and expiry date.

Reduce weak fallbacks safely

Review SMS, email links, recovery questions and legacy password-only routes. Restrict fallback methods based on risk, but do not disable alternatives before application coverage and support are ready. Measure enrollment, successful sign-in, fallback frequency, recovery events and authentication failures. Test phishing scenarios and legacy downgrade paths.

  • Inventory users, applications and privileged accounts.
  • Confirm identity-provider and application support.
  • Pilot enrollment, backup and recovery.
  • Set staged enforcement and exception expiry dates.
  • Monitor adoption, recovery and bypass attempts.
