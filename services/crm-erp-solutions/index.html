CRM & ERP Development and Integration

A CRM or ERP should reflect how a business actually works. We help teams understand their workflows, select a suitable direction, configure or build the required capabilities and connect systems without losing sight of data ownership or user adoption.

Start with the workflow, not the software

Before discussing a product or platform, map how information enters the business, who owns it, what decisions follow and where work gets delayed. A sales process may begin with a website enquiry and continue through qualification, quotation, approval, fulfilment and after-sales support. An operations process may connect purchasing, stock, service requests, billing and reporting. A useful CRM or ERP scope describes those steps before selecting screens or modules.

CRM capabilities we can help shape

CRM work can include lead and contact management, opportunity stages, activity history, reminders, follow-up automation, account ownership, service requests, customer communication preferences and management dashboards. We can review lead capture from websites and forms, define duplicate-handling rules, and connect approved email, messaging or business applications through supported integrations. The purpose is to give teams a consistent view of customer interactions—not to create more administration.

ERP and operational system improvements

ERP-related requirements may include inventory visibility, purchasing workflows, order processing, approval paths, service management, operational reporting and integration between departments. Not every business needs a large enterprise platform. Sometimes a focused workflow application or a small integration is more appropriate. The architecture should reflect transaction volume, control requirements, reporting needs and the maturity of existing systems.

Integration, migration and data quality

System connections need explicit ownership and rules. Define which system is authoritative for each record, how identifiers are matched, what happens when updates conflict and how failures are retried. Before migration, profile the data, identify duplicates, agree field mappings and test representative records. Keep an auditable migration plan and validate totals and key relationships after import. Avoid moving every legacy field merely because it exists.

Permissions, adoption and reporting

Role-based access, least privilege, retention requirements and secure integration credentials should be part of the design. Teams also need a practical onboarding plan: clear field definitions, short task-based guidance, agreed process ownership and a route to report problems. Dashboards should focus on decisions—such as pipeline health, overdue actions or service workload—rather than displaying every available metric.

A sensible delivery sequence

A CRM or ERP engagement can move through discovery, workflow mapping, platform fit assessment, configuration or development, integration, data migration, user acceptance testing and handover. We agree scope and responsibilities before implementation. Platform licensing, third-party subscriptions and vendor-specific services are evaluated separately and are not assumed to be included in a development engagement.

Possible deliverables

  • Workflow and requirements map
  • CRM/ERP configuration or custom modules
  • API and system integration plan
  • Data mapping, migration rehearsal and validation
  • Role permissions and user acceptance test cases
  • Documentation and handover guidance

The scope, timeline, fees, platform choices and responsibilities are confirmed in a project-specific proposal. Third-party software licences or subscriptions are separate unless the written scope says otherwise.

To discuss a requirement, share the current process, systems involved, users, constraints and the outcome you want to improve.
